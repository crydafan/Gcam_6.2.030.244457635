# Memoir

There was a time when GCam modding was a huge part of my life.

Back then, I spent countless hours working on Google Camera ports, mostly focused on bringing support to low-end Samsung Exynos devices. It wasn't glamorous work, and it certainly wasn't easy, but I loved every moment of it. Looking back now, those years feel distant—almost like memories from another lifetime. Reviving this project is my way of revisiting that chapter, preserving a small piece of something that meant a great deal to me.

The world of GCam modding was very different in those days. Most modifications were done directly in Smali, painstakingly editing decompiled Android bytecode line by line. It was tedious, frustrating, and often overwhelming, but it was also how many of us learned. Even today, a surprising amount of reverse engineering still requires working at that level.

As the community evolved, so did the tools and techniques we used. I remember when Arnova began experimenting with a different workflow during the development of his Google Camera 8.1 mod—decompiling portions of GCam back into Java, making changes there, and then converting the result back into Smali for integration. If memory serves me right, Zoran had already explored a similar idea with ZGCAM using Eclipse, while Arnova adopted Android Studio.

I followed a similar path and spent years developing mods in Android Studio. At the time, it felt like the natural choice. But life moved on. I became a software engineer, and Visual Studio Code gradually became the tool I used for almost everything. One day, I wondered what a modern GCam modding workflow would look like if it were built around the tools I use today. That simple question eventually became this project.

More than anything, though, this repository is a tribute to a period of my life that I remember fondly.

I often think about myself at fifteen years old, sitting in front of a computer late at night, trying to make sense of endless walls of Smali code. I didn't know what I was doing most of the time. I was learning through trial and error, reading forums, breaking things, fixing them, and staying awake far longer than I should have because I couldn't stop chasing the answer to the next problem. Looking back, I realize that many of the skills I use professionally today were born during those sleepless nights.

I also wouldn't have made it very far without the people who helped me along the way.

Greatness was my mentor when I was just getting started. He taught me far more than he probably realizes and helped transform a curious teenager into someone who could actually contribute to the community. Later, onFire helped me sharpen my modding skills and pushed me to keep learning. When I eventually found myself diving into ARM reverse engineering, Wyroczen became both a mentor and a friend, patiently guiding me through unfamiliar territory while somehow finding time to joke around and make the whole process enjoyable.

The older I get, the more I realize that what made those years special wasn't just the code. It was the people, the excitement of discovery, the late-night conversations, the feeling that there was always something new to learn, and the sense of belonging to a community of people who were just as obsessed as I was.

Those days are gone now, and I know they aren't coming back. Time moves forward, and so do we. But some memories never really leave us. They stay quietly in the background, shaping who we become.

This project exists because of those memories.

And for that, I will always be grateful.

To everyone who decides to follow along, contribute, or simply take a look around: thank you.
